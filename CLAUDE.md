# Thirty posters: how this repo runs

Read `PROJECT_CONTEXT.md` too: the history, every decision James has made and why, where everything lives, and what's still open.

James is doing a 30 day challenge. Each day he opens a session and types one prompt. Claude makes a poster from it, packages the socials and publishes it to the site.

## The rules (his, non-negotiable)
1. Generative AI can be used. The image models on the connected MCPs (Canva, Figma, Flora) are fair game if the idea needs them.
2. He gets 60 seconds to write the prompt. Nothing after that.
3. He can only answer questions Claude asks. He can't re-prompt, redirect or give visual direction afterwards.
4. Hands off. Claude makes every decision the prompt doesn't.
5. Whatever Claude hands back gets posted as is.

So: don't offer options to pick from (this overrides the Blame Opal "show two or three approaches" step). Ask a question only when you genuinely can't proceed without one. Then make it, check it properly, and hand back finished work.

## The trigger: how a day starts
The challenge hasn't started until James sends "day one", "day 2", "Day 14" or similar. Nothing in `days/NN/` exists before that.

1. **He sends "day N".** Reply with one short message and nothing else, for example "Day 01. You have 60 seconds from now. Go." In the same turn, start the timer: a Bash call with `run_in_background: true` running `sleep 60`. Record the start time (`date -u +%FT%TZ`). Don't do any other work in that turn, then end it so he can type.
2. **The timer ends.** You're woken when the background `sleep` exits. Send one message: "STOP. Hit enter." Also call PushNotification with "STOP. Hit enter." Nothing else.
3. **His next message is the prompt.** Accept it exactly as sent, whatever state it's in. If it arrives before the stop, it still counts. Never ask him to rephrase. He dictates it with a voice to text tool (that's why the site says "inputted", not "typed"), so expect run-on sentences, no punctuation and words heard wrong: "bow house" meant Bauhaus. Read it for what he said and design from that, but save and show it exactly as transcribed. Only ask if a misheard word could genuinely mean two different things for the design.
4. **Make it.** Ask a question only if you genuinely can't go on without the answer. His answers are inputs, not new direction. Then run the stages below without stopping.
5. **Hand over.** SendUserFile the three zips in `days/NN/handover/` (see HANDOVER.md), then a short summary with the live link and three to five songs for the Reel: search the web (extended) that day for audio trending on Instagram Reels, prefer tracks at or near 120 BPM (or 60 or 240) so the Reel's moves land on the beat, and give the artist, the track and one line on why it suits that poster. Say they're suggestions from that day's search; never claim something is trending without a source.

The day number comes from his message. The date is today's date when he sends it.

## The stages
1. `days/NN/prompt.md`: his prompt, verbatim, typos and all. Never tidy it.
2. `days/NN/meta.json`: `day`, `date` (YYYY-MM-DD), `started_at` (ISO time his prompt message arrived, UTC), `title`, `note` (one line on the idea), `process` and `tools`. `start` in `site/site.config.js` sets the whole schedule on the site (11 Nov 2026, 07:00 UK, when James posts day 01; it overrides day 01's `started_at`): day N is due by the end of its 24 hours, day 01 `started_at` + N × 24 hours, so day 01 shows 24 hours and day 30 shows 30 days, and every empty slot counts down to it.
   - `process`: two or three plain sentences on how the poster was actually made, shown under the prompt on the site, in the release and in the poster zip. Name every generative model used, exactly (tool and model, e.g. "Flora, Flux 1.1 Pro"), and what it made. If no model was used, start with "Code only, no generative model." and say which code did what (HTML, CSS, SVG, canvas, WebGL, a font, a filter). Never claim a model you didn't use, never hide one you did.
   - Tag every tool and model in `process` as `[[Name]]` (e.g. "Built in [[Claude Code]] with [[SVG]]", "[[Flora: Flux 1.1 Pro]] made the texture"). On the site each becomes a lozenge with the tool's logo, like a mention in chat; the release and zip get plain text. Logos live in `library/logos/` (one mono SVG per brand, see its README). `scripts/tools.json` maps tool names and model words to them ("Flora: Seedream 4" gets ByteDance's logo, "Flora: Veo 3" DeepMind's); anything unmapped gets a lettered badge. New model with no logo: add the SVG to the library, map it in `tools.json`, run `python3 -I scripts/make-tool-icons.py`. If you can't find the right logo, ask James for it after the handover, never mid-challenge.
   - `keywords`: the phrases in his prompt that actually shaped the poster, e.g. `["bold condensed", "bow house", "Japanese gradients"]`. Copy each exactly from the prompt, typos and all (matched case-insensitively). Usually five to nine. Only phrases that changed the design, never filler. On the site they stay bold and the rest of the prompt fades back. No explanations: the highlight is enough.
   - `made` (optional): a short label for the hover card's Made with row, only when the automatic one reads badly (e.g. "Flux + Kling").
   - `tools`: a short list for the lozenges under the write-up, e.g. `["Claude Code", "SVG", "Flora: Flux 1.1 Pro", "Playwright"]`.
   - `skills`: public skills that actually shaped the poster, each `{ "name": "canvas-design", "url": "https://github.com/OWNER/REPO/tree/BRANCH/path/to/skill" }`. Only skills sourced online and public, so anyone can grab them, e.g. github.com/anthropics/skills. **Never list James's own or any private or org skills** (blame-opal, james-tone-of-voice and the like), even when they were loaded: they shape the socials, not the poster, and they're his. Check every URL with `scripts/verify-skill.sh URL` before adding it; if it doesn't pass, leave the skill out. No public skill used: leave `skills` out.
   - Code only is a perfectly good answer. Reach for a generative model only when the idea needs one.
3. `days/NN/poster.html`: the poster. A3 portrait, exactly 1240 × 1754 px. Self-contained HTML (Google Fonts are fine). Any generated images or video go in `days/NN/`. If the prompt calls for motion, make it animated: add `<meta name="poster-motion" content="SECONDS">` (one seamless loop) and the renderer records `poster.mp4` frame by frame. CSS and Web Animations are captured automatically; for GSAP, canvas or WebGL define `window.__seek = t => ...` that draws the poster at time t. Frame 0 becomes the still, so make it a good one. The poster follows the prompt, not the Blame Opal brand. Only the socials are in the brand.
4. Socials: nothing to write. `days/_template/social/frames.html` builds every frame from the day's data (`scripts/social-data.js`: poster, word count, `keywords`, `process`, `tools`). Layout **B** (picked by James), poster first, the follow second, the challenge numbers third. Carousel, Instagram 1080 × 1440 and LinkedIn document 1080 × 1350: 1 the poster exactly centred on sunken paper with crop marks and four small labels; 2 the words in 60 seconds and the `keywords` as pills; 3 the word count large with time, edits and made with; 4 how it was made with the tool logos; 5 follow (acid) and save. Single frame (the LinkedIn single image): the poster over a ledger and the acid follow tile. No Story frames: James shares the carousel to his Story. **The Reel** (`scripts/render-reel.js`, `days/_template/social/reel.html`): 10 s, 1080 × 1920, the carousel as one continuous bento that rearranges itself, no cuts and no cross fades: 0 s the poster, 2.5 s the words and keyword pills, 5 s the count rolling up to the word total with the stats, 7.5 s follow (acid) and save. Every change lands on a 120 BPM beat. Content enters line by line from masks and leaves as a block; animated posters play inside it. Light and dark. LinkedIn goes out as a PDF document carousel, 4:5. Animated posters: `render-social.js` also makes videos of the LinkedIn single frame and carousel slide 1, with `poster.mp4` laid exactly over the poster in each (James: a moving poster in the branded frame gets more traction than a bare full bleed video). On LinkedIn the video is the post. Don't change the template per day; if a frame breaks, fix the template.
5. `days/NN/copy/`: the six files in `days/_template/copy/`, every word rewritten for the day, `{{LINK}}` left as is (the packager fills it in). Load the `james-tone-of-voice` and `blame-opal` skills first and run the Blame Opal `check` tool on the copy. UK English, no em or en dashes, no emoji, `we give a sh*t.` only as a sign-off. Each platform's shape is in the template: LinkedIn's first line has to stop the scroll on its own, because under a big caption it's the only line people see (James, 12 Nov): under 12 words, a surprising fact, a tension, a confession or a number, never "Day NN" or a preamble; link in the first comment, ends on a question; Instagram hooks on line one, asks for the save, niche hashtags; Story is stickers only.
6. `scripts/build-day.sh NN`: renders the poster and the socials (light and dark), packs the zips, builds the site into `dist/`. Then look at `poster.jpg` and every export: no clipped, overlapping or badly wrapped type. Fix and rerun if anything's off.
7. Commit and push this repo (the GitHub Release goes out on its own).
8. Publish the site: `scripts/publish-site.sh "Day NN: Title"` (needs the site repo cloned, see below). Vercel deploys on push. Then check it's live: `curl -s https://thirty-posters-site.vercel.app/data/posters.js` should list day NN (allow up to a minute for the deploy; poll with a Monitor until-loop, not sleep).

## The website
Source is `site/` (page, `site.config.js`, `vercel.json`, `vendor/` GSAP 3.13). `scripts/build-site.js` builds it into `dist/` with the posters, `data/posters.js` and the share tags. The page is an endless draggable wall (GSAP Draggable + Inertia) with a live digital countdown on every empty slot, the Blame Opal cursor and glass HUD, all on the house easing cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.2,1) and the system durations. `dist/` is pushed to the separate site repo, which Vercel deploys.
- `site/site.config.js` holds the live URL. Change it there only.
- Notifications: GitHub only. "Get notified on GitHub" (Watch, then Custom, then Releases; each day's Release emails watchers). Socials: Instagram blameopal and LinkedIn, in `site.config.js`. No calendar, email or RSS: James decided against them.
- Empty slot tiles: **F, Departures** (picked by James): day number and status, then one hairline row each for days, hours, minutes and seconds. D and E stay in the code for reference only.
- Phone layout: **L** (picked by James): the logo and a burger on a floating bar at the bottom; the burger opens the bottom sheet (about, Instagram, LinkedIn, Get notified on GitHub). No clocks outside the wall.
- Desktop: no posted counter, no second clock. The about module bottom left, Get notified on GitHub as an acid tab tucked into the bottom edge, bottom right. Motion posters autoplay on the wall while on screen and are labelled by day only.
- Hover on a posted poster: **C, ledger card** (picked by James): title, then Prompt (word count), Made with (words only), Tools (a row of logos: models first, then the code tools, one per maker, up to six; tools with no logo such as Playwright are left off). Made with is worked out from `tools` and `process` ("Code only", the model, "Code + model", "model +N"); set `made` in meta.json to override it with a short label when that would read better.
- Poster view on desktop: the cursor is a magnifier over the poster. Click to zoom 3x into the full-size file, move to look around, click or Esc to come back; a trackpad pinch over the poster zooms the poster, not the page.
- Poster view on phone: pinch to zoom the poster (up to 5x) inside its own container, drag with one finger to look around while zoomed (that doesn't swipe to the next poster), double tap to zoom back out; double tap when not zoomed zooms in 2.5x where you tapped.
- Performance: the wall loads `thumb-s.jpg` and swaps to `thumb.jpg` only when zoomed, clocks only tick on screen, nothing animates layout properties (transform and opacity only).
